# Position and value bounds for De Vlieger’s digital-root permutation

With zero-based indexing, the term at position $n$ is at most $10n+19$, and every nonnegative integer $x$ appears by position $900x+992$. These are explicit bounds in both directions for the version in the OEIS table and program, in which the next term must contain the immediately preceding term’s digital root. [OEIS A340138](https://oeis.org/A340138)

Michael De Vlieger proposed the permutation in 2021. A complete proof of bijectivity was already public before this work began. The quantitative result here uses finite counts of requests. [Earlier Lean proof](https://github.com/blakestone-x/open-math-agents/blob/21379df6e8616b6782549bfe7ea4e9c142631458/proofs/oeis-a340138.lean) · [Earlier manuscript](https://github.com/blakestone-x/math/blob/8aa1a2147fbce9c321e96e6a90c3099805f2e495/Greedy-digit-permutations/decimal-permutations.tex)

Suppose a positive integer $y$ containing digit $d$ has yet to appear. Each request for $d$ must have selected a distinct number below $y$, so there can have been at most $y$ such requests. To force the appearance of $x>0$, choose a nonzero digit $r$ of $x$, put $L=90x+99$, and examine the first $10L+1$ requests. Some digit $d$ occurred more than $L$ times. Every positive number at most $L$ that contains $d$ has therefore appeared. Among them are $x+1$ different numbers ending in $d$ whose digital root is $r$. They ensure enough requests for $r$ to force $x$ by the stated index. In the other direction, one integer out of every ten has any prescribed units digit. The least-unused rule therefore also bounds the value chosen at each position.

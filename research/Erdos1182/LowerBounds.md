# Finite and general bounds for Erdős 1182

The public definitions of `Erdos1182.F`, `Erdos1182.f`, and `Erdos1182.IsTriangleGood` come from the pinned Formal Conjectures article. The R2 carrier has SHA-256 `a39b5a5695c95f51d95605e13c3079e323a015e65f3b8c226f05f2a9b58dc745`. In the statements below, `Cert n m` means one literal finite maximal-triangle-free host certificate from R2.H2. It concerns hosts with 2n−1 vertices. The mask records missing edges.

- `Results.lean` proves `ten_values_from_secondary`. The certificate for mask 72057645653176374 alone entails F(10)=18 and f(10)=33. The sparse classification in R2.H1 had placed every 18-edge graph in one of two templates. Reusing its earlier intermediate classification gives 17 possible terminal graphs for the second template. Every triangle-free coloring on the ten chosen vertices uses a subgraph of one of five maximal colorings permitted by that template. The 85 checked vertex permutations cover all 17 terminal graphs in each of those five cases. The primary 10-vertex certificate therefore drops from this implication. The secondary host certificate itself remains an input.
- `NineExact.lean` proves both F(9)=16 and f(9)=27 from the single 9-vertex certificate. `AlternativeMasks.lean` proves f(10)=33 and f(11)=41 from either of two certificates at the corresponding order. Either 49-edge template certifies f(12)=49. Either 48-edge template certifies 48≤f(12)≤49.
- `Range.lean` proves `remaining_sparse`. R2.H1 is equivalent to the restricted predicate for orders 11 and 12. The order-9 and order-10 sparse placement proofs already pass the kernel.
- `Growth.lean` proves `f_succ` and `f_growth` unconditionally on orders n≥2. Appending a pendant edge extends each good connected graph by one vertex and one edge. In a triangle-free red host on 2n+1 vertices, either a red neighborhood contains a blue (n+1)-clique, or the attachment vertex has a fresh blue neighbor. `LowerRange.lean` uses the order-eight witness to prove n+12≤f(n) for every n≥8.
- The original BBH templates and sparse thresholds come from Brandt, Brinkmann and Harmuth, *All Ramsey numbers r(K₃,G) for connected graphs of order 9* (1998). The finite order-eight proof replays the relevant part of KoaOkano's OMA #13482. The existing order-9/10 and upper-table verifiers were reused. `extract_eight.py` specifies the exact extraction; the old source emits proof-style linter warnings when compiled.
- `verification.json` records input hashes, checked local sources and axiom audits. `check.py MODULE` replays one module against the original workspace dependencies in `paths`, using the guarded second lane. The separate exact-case assemblies avoid the shared `OMA.solution` declaration name in previously verified component files.
